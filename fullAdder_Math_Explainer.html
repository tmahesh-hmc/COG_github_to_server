Building a Full Adder!

How Does the Binary Addition Work?

Introduction

Welcome, everybody! Before you physically construct your hardware, we must first understand the math that our hardware is able to compute.

A Full Adder, like the one we are making, has 3 inputs:

  • Input A
  • Input B
  • The Cin (Carry-in) value.

For this practical demonstration, think of Cin as just another digit, like A and B. However, in larger contraptions (like a ripple carry adder), this value is taken from the carry-out of the previous full adder.

Our example

To demonstrate, we shall observe what occurs when we input the decimal (or our "normal" number system) digits 110 into our Full Adder.

  • A = 1 (Top Input)
  • B = 1 (Middle Input)
  • Cin = 0 (Bottom Input)
⚙ ⚙ ⚙

Using the XOR gates to Calculate the "Sum" Component of the Binary Answer

Our device first employs two XOR (Exclusive OR) gates to compute the "Sum" part of the output.

XOR Gates: If the inputs are the same number (e.g., 0,0 or 1,1), the output is 0. If the inputs are different numbers (e.g., 0,1 or 1,0), the output is 1.

Step A: The first XOR gate accepts the initial decimal digits, A (1) and B (1). According to XOR logic, since they are the same number, it outputs:

1 +xor 1 = 0

Step B: The second XOR gate accepts this 0 output from Step A and our third input, Cin (0). As both inputs are 0 (same number), XOR logic produces a final Sum of:

0 +xor 0 = 0

And so, we determine that the bit on the right-hand side (Sum) of our final answer is 0.

⚙ ⚙ ⚙

Using AND and OR Gates to Calculate the "Carry-Out" Component

Next, the adder activates two AND gates to process the "Carry-out" part of the output.

AND Gate Logic: This gate produces a 1 output ONLY when BOTH inputs are 1. Any other scenario results in 0.

For our example, the first AND gate is fed inputs A (1) and B (1). Since they are both 1, the gate produces an output of 1.

The second AND gate is fed Cin (0) and the output of the FIRST XOR gate (which was 0). Since they are both 0, the output is 0.

Lastly, the full adder employs a single OR gate for the final Carry-out summation.

OR Gate Logic: This gate produces a 1 if any or all inputs are 1. A 0 is produced only if both inputs are 0.

This single OR gate accepts the results from our two AND gates: 1 and 0. Since there is a 1 present, the OR law gives us a final Carry-out output of:

1 +or 0 = 1

And so, we determine that the high-order bit (Carry-out) of our final answer is 1.

⚙ ⚙ ⚙

Conclusion of the Proof

We input A=1, B=1, and Cin=0. Our initial mathematical hypothesis was 1+1+0=2 (or '10' in binary).

Reading our outputs in order: Carry-out first, then Sum, we see that our mechanized adder produced the values:

1 0

Indeed! 1+1+0 = 10binary. The full adder logic seems complicated -- and it is! Congrats on making it through. These logic gates make up the most fundamental parts of computers!
